The background work (`ToneMatch.plan`) never touches the main thread, the semaphore is signalled after `plan` returns on every path (a crash inside it crashes the app; it can't leave the wait hanging), and the semaphore never drops below its starting value, so freeing it is safe. `finishPendingTone` can't re-enter: `land` clears `pendingTone` before it does anything else, and the main-queue `land` that arrives later sees it's no longer pending. Closing and quitting are the gap (finding 2).

## 1. A big Clone Stamp stroke on a big image freezes Colorbee for many seconds if you do anything before its Match Tone is ready
- **Severity:** High (freeze; a tester may Force Quit and lose unsaved work)
- **Confidence:** High that it blocks; Medium on the exact times (measured on a scratch copy of the code, then scaled up)
- **Where:** `Sources/Colorbee/Editor.swift:1757-1761` (`finishPendingTone` waits on the main thread) with `Packages/ColorbeeCore/Sources/ColorbeeCore/RetouchStroke.swift:451-467` (the full-size relaxing in `smoothFill`), `:383` (`scale`), and the arrays at `:326-327`, `:355`, `:398`, `:432`, `:452-454`
- **What happens:** Since review R's fix, anything that starts a new edit waits for a pending Match Tone, on the main thread. That's fine while the tone takes a fraction of a second, but on a big image it can take tens of seconds. Until it's done, clicking to paint, pressing a tool key, ⌘Z, switching layer or page, or ⌘S shows the spinning wheel and macOS may report Colorbee as not responding. Before the fix, the app stayed usable (the tone was dropped instead). The tone also takes that long to appear when you just wait.
  - **Time.** The last step of `smoothFill` relaxes *every* pixel inside the stroke (not only "near the edge", as its comment says), `2 × scale` times, on one core, and `scale` grows with the square root of the stroke's pixel count. So the time grows as (stroke pixels)^1.5. In the scratch copy (Release, this Mac), a stroke covering a 2000 × 2000 area took 1.4 s and 3000 × 3000 took 4.0 s, nearly all in that step. Scaled up: about 9 s for 4000 × 4000, about 20 s for most of an 8000 × 5000 image, about 45 s for most of a 48 MP iPhone photo, and about 70 s for 8000 × 8000. A single 300 px stroke corner to corner of an 8000 × 8000 image takes only about 1 s.
  - **Memory.** Most of `plan`'s arrays are sized to the stroke's whole bounding box, not the stroke: `offsets`, its copy `raw`, and `smoothFill`'s `result` are each 16 bytes per pixel of the box (1 GB each at 8000 × 8000), plus `weights`, `known`, `onEdge`, `isCell`, and `cells` and `loose` at 16 bytes per stroke pixel. The scratch copy peaked at 1.0 GB for a 3000 × 3000 stroke (about 110 bytes per pixel), so a stroke covering 8000 × 8000 needs about 7 GB at once. Even the single diagonal stroke needs about 4 GB, because its box is the whole image. A thin stroke (Size 1–4) is worse still: with under 40,000 stroke pixels, `scale` is 1, so the "coarse" grid (`sums`, `grid`) is full-size too, about 2 GB more. The finished plan (1 GB of offsets plus the cells) is kept until it lands.
- **How to trigger it:** Test file: `TestImages/Stage 9 Practice Photo.png`. Use a Release build (`make run`).
  1. Open the photo. Choose Image ▸ Resize and Skew…, set it to 500% (8000 × 5000), and click OK.
  2. Choose View ▸ Zoom to Fit.
  3. Choose the Retouch tool ▸ Clone Stamp. Set Size to 300 and turn on Match Tone.
  4. Option-click on the photo to pick a source.
  5. Paint one long stroke, back and forth across the photo, until it covers most of the image. Then let go.
  6. Straight away, click on the photo to paint another dab (or press ⌘Z).
  7. Expected: Colorbee responds at once. Actual: the spinning wheel for roughly 10–25 seconds before anything happens. In Activity Monitor, Colorbee's memory jumps by several GB while it works.
- **Why:** `finishPendingTone` is `pending.done.wait()` then `land(pending)`, called first thing in `finishInteractions` and `placePendingObjects`. The work it waits for runs `for _ in 0..<(2 * scale) { for (fx, fy) in loose { … } }`, where `loose` is every unknown stroke pixel, and `scale = ⌈√(cells / 40,000)⌉`. Arrays like `var offsets = [SIMD4<Float>](repeating: .zero, count: width * height)` use the box's `width * height`, and `let raw = offsets` followed by writes to `offsets` makes a second full copy.
- **Fix idea (for the engineer):** keep the full-size pass to a band a few blocks wide beside the edge, as its comment intends, and spread it across cores in row bands. Size the coarse grid by the box, not the cell count (so it's never full-size), and keep per-pixel arrays to the stroke (or reuse one buffer instead of `raw` and `result`). If a tone can still take more than a moment, the main thread shouldn't wait for it unbounded.
- **Test that would catch it:** a Release-only `make perf` test: `ToneMatch.plan` for a filled 4000 × 4000 mask with a reference finishes within about 1 s, and for a 3 px diagonal mask across 8000 × 8000 without allocating full-size coarse grids. An app test: with a pending tone that takes long, `beginStroke` returns within a frame or two.

## 2. Closing the window or quitting right after a big stroke saves it without its matched tone
- **Severity:** Medium (the saved image isn't what Match Tone would have shown; no warning)
- **Confidence:** High
- **Where:** `Sources/Colorbee/ImageDocument.swift:165` (`saveSnapshot(placingPending: !isAutosave)`), `Sources/Colorbee/Editor.swift:4100-4101` (only `placePendingObjects` calls `finishPendingTone`)
- **What happens:** Closing a document that has a file, or quitting, saves it by autosave. Autosave deliberately doesn't place a shape or text still being edited, and the same switch also skips waiting for a pending Match Tone. The file gets the stroke without its tone, the window closes, and the tone is never applied. Reopening shows the copy in its own, unmatched brightness and color. ⌘S, Export and Share do wait, so they're fine; so does the "Save changes?" sheet for an untitled image.
- **How to trigger it:** Test file: a copy of `TestImages/Stage 9 Practice Photo.png` (in Finder, Option-drag it to the Desktop, so the original isn't changed). Use a Release build.
  1. Open the copy. Choose Image ▸ Resize and Skew…, set it to 500%, and click OK. Press ⌘S.
  2. Choose View ▸ Zoom to Fit. Choose Retouch ▸ Clone Stamp, Size 300, Match Tone on.
  3. Option-click somewhere bright (the sky), then paint one long back-and-forth stroke across a darker part, covering about half the photo. Let go.
  4. Straight away, press ⌘W.
  5. Open the copy again.
  6. Expected: the stroke blended in (matched tone). Actual: the stroke shows the bright copied tone, as if Match Tone were off.
- **Why:** `saveSnapshot(placingPending:)` runs `if placingPending { placePendingObjects() }`, and `placePendingObjects` is the only save-side caller of `finishPendingTone()`. Close and quit go through NSDocument's autosave (`autosavesInPlace` is true), which sets `isAutosave`, so `placingPending` is false. The editor then goes away, and the `[weak self]` main-queue `land` finds nothing.
- **Fix idea (for the engineer):** wait for a pending tone before any save, autosave too: it doesn't need anything from the user, unlike a shape or text. With finding 1 fixed that wait is short. Otherwise, at least before closing and quitting.
- **Test that would catch it:** an app test: start a background tone (lower `backgroundToneArea`), take `saveSnapshot(placingPending: false)`, and check the snapshot holds the toned pixels.

## 3. Match Tone tints the soft rim too strongly where the Clone Stamp copies partly see-through pixels onto solid ones
- **Severity:** Low (edge case)
- **Confidence:** Medium (the arithmetic is certain; how visible it is depends on the image)
- **Where:** `Packages/ColorbeeCore/Sources/ColorbeeCore/RetouchStroke.swift:480` (`let weight = p.a == 0 ? 0 : min(1, Float(mask[x, y]) / Float(p.a))`), with `CoveragePainter.lerp` (`CoveragePainter.swift:185-193`)
- **What happens:** The weight is meant to be "how much of the pixel's color is the copy". The stroke mixes the copy in with premultiplied weights, so the copy's real share is coverage × copy's opacity ÷ the result's opacity. The code leaves out the copy's opacity, so it's exact only when the copy is fully solid. Where the copy is partly see-through, the rim gets too much correction, up to the full amount. Where it's fully see-through (the Clone Stamp copying empty space onto a solid layer, which erases), the rim pixels keep their original color, yet they still get shifted by up to the full correction. That leaves a faint tinted ring around that part of the stroke. Example: an opaque pixel, a copy at 0% opacity, coverage 50%: the result is the original color at 50% opacity, and the weight is 128 ÷ 128 = 1.
- **How to trigger it:** Test file: `TestImages/Stage 9 Practice Cutout.png` (a subject on a see-through background). This may be faint by hand; the unit test below is the sure check.
  1. Open the cutout. Choose Retouch ▸ Clone Stamp, Size 150, Hardness 0%, Match Tone on, Sample All Layers off.
  2. Option-click on the subject close to a light part of its outline, so part of the brush's source lies on the see-through background.
  3. Paint one stroke across a darker part of the subject.
  4. Turn Match Tone off, press ⌘Z, and paint the same stroke again to compare.
  5. Expected: where the brush copied the empty background, the subject fades softly with its own colors. Actual: with Match Tone on, that soft fade has a light (or tinted) ring that isn't there with Match Tone off.
- **Why:** with `t` the coverage, `lerp` gives alpha `wa + wb` with `wb = copy.a × t`, so the copy's share is `wb / alpha`. The code uses `255 × t / alpha`, which is larger by `255 / copy.a`.
- **Test that would catch it:** a core test: a 1-row opaque layer, a plan with a non-zero offset, painted with a copy at alpha 0 and coverage 128. `ToneMatch.apply` must leave the color unchanged, and with a copy at alpha 128 it must shift by about a third of the offset, not two thirds.
